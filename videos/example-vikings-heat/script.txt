Somewhere in Iceland more than a thousand years ago, a family is asleep on a winter night. Outside, the wind is strong enough to strip the skin off an unprotected face, and the temperature has fallen to minus 30 degrees Celsius, maybe lower. Inside, under a roof made of grass, the air is warm enough to sleep without furs pulled over your head.
There's no chimney pumping heat through pipes. Nothing that looks by modern standards like a heating system at all. And yet the house works.
It worked for centuries. In a few places it still does. The people who built it left behind no engineering diagrams and no owner manuals.
